My Accidental Wish

By Aaron Ebeling · July 2024

It would really be nice to have the option of wishing to turn back time, or wish for everyone to forget something about you, or even just wish for a million dollars so you could move after being embarrassed. But no, I live in a world where genies aren’t all-powerful and I’m beginning to question if one of their wishes they can grant is a hoax. The only wishes that genies can actually grant are to bring someone back from the dead for a day or to have someone fall in love with you for a day, or so they say.

Hi, my name is Jason and almost every day for the three and a half months I’ve wished that Angela… well, that Angela would like… like me. And my genie lamp keeps saying that the wish has been granted. But, she and I have yet to talk. I guess wishing for someone to “love” you doesn’t include a relationship.

So yesterday I’d made a plan with my great great great uncle Regenold for how I could get the ball rolling. Even though great great great uncle Regenold is out of touch with the times as he has been dead for the past 30 years everyone still comes to him for relationship advice.

I was prepared with:

  1. A game (the reason I could start a conversation with her)
  2. My conversation tool (to help me remember what I could talk about if I ever froze up)
  3. A flower, (stuffed deeply in my backpack covered in a wet paper towel and a plastic bag. In case, well in case things went really well)

The conversation tool was key. I didn’t have much experience talking to girls, really just people in general. I really wanted her to like me so, I pre-wrote whole conversations. Each question had follow-up questions that branched down. Some questions I wrote were just a bit too daring to ask. Like “hey, do you want to go on a date? Or do you like me? Or have you ever wished me to like you?”

I had just come out of class heading to free period, game and conversation kit in hand, when I saw Angela walk out of class. Everything was going to plan! As I walked closer, my anxiety felt like a gorilla was hugging me but I made it all the way to her without looking like a total fool.

My hand was just about to touch her, when out of nowhere, I was yanked around. It was so unexpected that I dropped the game and more importantly my conversation kit.

The kit fell in slow motion right next to Mike, who picked up the conversation notes. There was a moment when I looked him in the eye silently pleading that he wouldn’t look too closely at it. I reached toward the conversation kit. But Mike held it out of my reach and started looking through it. His eyes widened and then glinted when he shouted.

“Hey! You want to know how awkward Jason is, he’s got whole conversations planned out… he even has branching trees of questions.”

The hall quieted as Mike filtered through the pages to where it started getting more and more personal. When he said,

“Hey Angela, have you ever wished for Jason to love you from your lamp, ’cause he sure has.”

The hall burst out laughing and like the cowardly person I am, I ran. I didn’t go for my conversation kit, didn’t look at Angela to see what her reaction was, I didn’t even think how she could feel about it, I didn’t clean up my game splattered around the hallway, I didn’t stand up for myself.

Mike was probably looking through the rest of the conversation tree making fun of every little bit for the whole school to hear. You ask, what did I actually do? I hid in a janitoral closet hoping that no one would need cleaning supplies for the next year as I fully intended to starve to death. The smell of bleach stung my nose and eyes as I sat and did what I do best, make the situation a big deal.

The school day wasn’t even over, and I had class to get to. When the bell rang signaling the end of free period, I could probably have just gone out and made it all look like not that big of a deal.

But oh no, surprise! I was going to make it worse. I decided to stay in my janitorial closet and ended up missing class. The smell of bleach was my only companion. It was going to have been a good day. I felt my eyes water up and asked myself “Why am I so stupid?”

Since schools really need to know where their students are, my name was called on the intercom for the whole school to hear. I sat on a bucket and thought about leaving the closet just to stop the intercom but promptly dismissed the idea. The self-loathing changed then into a low level of anxiety. Surely, they wouldn’t find me here my plan to starve to death would still work.

But my hopes were dashed when the intercom came on again asking me to report to class. I could have gone then, but then everyone else would see me and my now red eyes and streaming nose. (Which were totally from the bleach and not my crushed dreams). But with no word of me and the school being into following rules of no kids getting lost, they started a search with teachers which I could hear slowly coming my way.

At what point did I actually make the appropriate response?

It certainly wasn’t that point. The fear of a teacher finding me hiding and anxious on the brink of a panic attack in a janitorial closet over such a small thing as being embarrassed over some conversation notes. It was unthinkable, so I locked the door as quietly as possible.

But you know locks they always have that really loud click at the end when the bolt shoots home. This was one of those locks.

Footsteps came closer and my previous anxiety that was so crushing wasn’t even on the scale. I surpassed it like an Olympian winning a freestyle race against a toddler.

The knock came and I held my breath.

The door rattled. The teacher said “Jason are you in there?”

In full panic mode, my mind working a million miles an hour I froze I couldn’t even move. The teacher muttered something about getting a key to check the door and the footsteps receded.

Now I wasn’t only going to be embarrassed in front of my peers but I had just annoyed my teachers and was being frustrating. I thought about making a break for it before the teacher came back, but when my hand reached towards the door, the bell rang letting class go for the next hour.

I looked around the room looking for a way to escape my fate and my shame when I saw a false ceiling. I felt better about climbing the rickety shelves full of cleaning chemicals than heading out into the hall and the noise from the hall would totally cover any noise I was making.

When I made it to the ceiling I had to stretch backward leaning precariously on the shelf trying fruitlessly to push the tile away so I could make my escape. It just wouldn’t budge. I heard a key go in the lock and turn making a final clicking sound. So I tried with a desperate push to get it open.

It didn’t budge anything above me but, shelves in a cleaning closet are not meant to hold a fifteen-year-old especially one that is jumping on the top rack desperate to leave.

As I fell breaking the shelf I was on I remember thinking there is no way I am ever going to live this down.

When the door opened there were still students and the one teacher who witnessed my fall and the fall of many hazardous cleaning materials, that of course had to break.

There is a first for everything. My dad says that a lot. Not sure if he thinks it’s funny. But when the school had to be evacuated and I needed to go to the emergency room to ensure I hadn’t consumed too many fumes it did feel like a defining day.

My parents took it well, but for me, I was in shock. I got a lecture on common sense and when to use it. I got a different one on embracing the embarrassment or why would you be embarrassed by that, another one on we all make mistakes and yet another on all the embarrassing things my parents had done.

With every story and conversation, I was like “why am I so stupid? Why did I let it get so bad? Just how could I have been that dumb?”

When I finally made it to my room. The lump of “well you’re never going to live this one down” settled comfortably into my gut. I slowly unpacked my backpack. It was starting to get a bleach stain along the side. My school books were mostly undamaged. My lunch was uneaten and I was just checking to make sure everything was gone from today. I remembered the flower. The flower I’d hidden just in case things went well something good I could give to Angela to remember me by.

I took off the plastic bag, and then the wet paper towel that was showing signs of some color. So it wasn’t too much of a surprise when the flower I’d so daringly taken from our garden was smooshed and utterly broken. Now that I’d thought about it putting the flower part in the paper towel and covering it with a plastic bag was probably the most damaging thing I could have done. I just didn’t want someone to accidentally see it for what it was.

Not sure why it was so important but it was a symbol of the dream where genie stories seemed to work out and everyone lived happily ever after.

I skipped dinner and laid awake that night far too late wondering how bad tomorrow would be and it turned out that I vastly overestimated it.

When I woke up from questionable sleep, it was to my grandfather’s glasses right in front of my face. Kind of like Voldemort looking down at Dumbledore’s tomb. Creepily so. That does tend to happen as a side effect of someone being brought back from the dead for the day. They don’t need social cues and can only interact in tiny ways with the physical world but all of them seem to get really good at looming.

I shouted and tried rolling away only to succeed in falling off of my bed and knocking my genie lamp off in the process. I hit hard which jolted me awake. My great great grandfather Regenold died about 60 years ago and I only knew him when he was brought back through a genie.

Regenold poked me with a translucent finger that went right into me and said, Jason, I’m going to lie to you and tell you that everything is going to be just as bad as you think it will.

Not how I expected the conversation to go but, because my mind was still just waking up I realized what actually happened yesterday. Sadly it wasn’t a bad dream that I could wake up from. It was real.

I groaned putting my arm across my face and then Regenold watched as I pulled my pillow off the bed so I could hide more effectively from the world.

Grandpa Regenold poked me again which felt a little like a jolt of electricity and said “Hey…… hey Jason you can’t hide from me. I’m literally a ghost and can go through things. I know you feel embarrassed and ya know I was embarrassed too in my life. But now that I’m dead it doesn’t really matter.

Never mind that part, I don’t think all the times I was embarrassed stayed with me in the same sense. I can remember what happened but I mean now it is a story a sign that I tried something… even if it didn’t work.

I poked an eye above my pillow with a raised eyebrow.

Grandpa Regenold said “Hey it’s not because I’m dead but it probably is because I’m old. I think it’s just that I have too many other things pushing the embarrassment out. It’s really about what you focus on. Now it’s going to be hard to not focus on that. Maybe I’m no longer embarrassed by things because I have failed in greater ways. But either way, your dad wished me here to make sure you used your common sense in the future. But I don’t reckon that you need advice like that.”

I didn’t know how to respond to that. So I just let my mind wander into how badly I’d messed up. Just remembering Mike’s reading of my conversation kit, over and over again, when I closed my eyes, I was back in the closet just waiting and wishing to not be myself.

Uncle Regenold spoke again, saying “I think you’ve got this. I know it probably doesn’t feel like it. But you got this.”

I waited a long while just breathing in and out wishing I was just better seeing all the possible options I could have taken but were now far too late. Why. Was I so stupid? Why did I make this into such a big deal? Why? why!! why?!!!. The question built up momentum and I grabbed at the pillow to crush it to exert some influence on the world around me. But the pillow just absorbed it and then when I was exhausted stretched back into its original shape.

In despair, the first words I uttered that day were “I just wish I liked myself.” The words rang in the room and the Genie lamp that was right next to me said.

“Thank you for your wish, it has been granted, You will get another wish in 24 hours, hope you have a wonderful day.”

For the second time that day, I startled and I startled hard. Fully awake I looked down at the genie lamp in wonder. I didn’t know I could wish for that. I almost thought, what a waste. But then my mind stopped me.

It was weird. I didn’t feel much better, but when I tried to spiral into my dark thoughts, my thoughts balked at the idea that this would define my life. I could think that what I’d done was dumb, but when I tried to say I was dumb the words just felt untrue. I could admit I’d made a mistake, but not feel that I was a mistake.

I actually felt able to go downstairs, where I saw my mom. She was on the computer looking at a spreadsheet when I walked into the kitchen. And I sat down next to her. She looked over and pulled me into a side hug. And we just sat for a time. My head on her shoulder. The silence wasn’t awkward, it was warm and comforting.

I said “Hey mom, thanks for being there for me. It must have been a stressful day for you. I really did make things worse I was caught up in the moment. But Mom, I love you.”

Mom just rubbed my shoulder and hugged me close. “I think you are doing so good Jason.” “How was your talk with Grandpa? I hope we didn’t over-talk you… well at least not too much.”

I snuggled in closer to my mom, “it was a bit much but thanks for sending in Regenold.”

After a little while I asked, “What is going to happen today?” It was a question I often asked, but today it held extra weight. I knew what I probably had to do. Just doing it sounded so difficult. Contemplating it made me want to curl up into a ball again.

She looked down at me and said “Well it’s a little bit up to you. What do you feel like you should do?”

That was the thing with Mom. She flipped things back on you and your conscience. What did I feel like I needed to do? I felt like I should apologize to the school, for causing an evacuation. I knew I probably should apologize to Angela for so many things. But it was probably what I least wanted to do. It reminded me that I had messed up. Not that I could feel too bad about myself at the moment. So it might be good to just get it over with. Certainly waiting had made things a lot worse yesterday and today was a new day.

“I think I should go to the school to see if I can apologize, or make amends somehow.”

I didn’t say that I wanted to talk to Angela and apologize to her especially. But with that, Mom said, “Why don’t you get dressed and we can leave in ten minutes.”

When I got back to my room to change clothes I grabbed a random shirt and some jeans. But once I got them on, I looked in the mirror and it felt off. It didn’t feel right I couldn’t imagine myself meeting the principal and Angela looking like that. I needed to be somber. or at least more …. Put together? Like someone who hadn’t just had a mental breakdown.

I tried the suit next, but that felt way worse. By the third outfit, I was frustrated, and worried out of my mind. Just meeting with people I’d failed really made me feel anxious. Why did I have to be such a loser… The thought again didn’t ring true.

And you know what I did? You know what I DID! I thought to myself, Nothing is gonna feel right, you are just nervous because you made a mistake and it’s new and uncomfortable. I felt the tension bleed out of my fingertips and I did change back to my first outfit because that was, or who I am at least for the moment. Jeans and a T-shirt.

It was great to have made the realization that I was just nervous and that I hadn’t become fashion-conscious overnight, but I still dreaded going to school.

The car ride was pretty uneventful as long as you don’t count me poking a hole in my jeans with my bare fingers through sheer brute force. I am pretty talented at worrying. But before I walked too far down memory lane, I was sitting in school shaking like there was an earthquake. It was my earthquake, but still, it shook my chair and the ground beneath me in a two-foot radius. Mom did put a hand on my leg to stop it from shaking but I started it up again as soon as her hand left.

The principal Mrs. Fieldsmithe towered over me with a frown on her face. And said “Come in” It was super intimidating. She definitely succeeded in her intimidation check, it was way above my armor class.

I was still sitting when I blurted out my first of thirty-eight apologies and, it was way too quiet. My mom looked at me and reached for my hand. Mrs. Fieldsmithe didn’t quite catch it and tried to lean in closer to hear better.

I could have stood up and enunciated my words loud enough for someone to hear. But I did the second worst thing, I said my apology again but in only a slightly louder voice. The adults were still recovering from not understanding the first so my second apology didn’t make it further than the first. And they had to ask for a second time what I had just said. But at that point, I wasn’t going to try again till we were close enough to hear, so I kept my mouth shut and gestured to the room. Which made me feel like I put the cherry on top of a mound of whipped cream that far outweighed the actual substance beneath it. I was inviting Mrs. Fieldsmithe into her own office. But, I wasn’t going to make the same mistake a third time.

It matters how you say things, it really does. And it really matters how you say things to yourself. I could have thought wow you are so dumb why couldn’t you have waited? But instead, today I thought “you were trying and it just didn’t work out the way you wanted.”

My apology did eventually make it across and everything was going to be all right. No harm was done. When we walked out of the office I felt so relieved like I was a balloon on a Ferris wheel.

Yes, I did say Ferris wheel. Because my heart was doing great until we left the office and started walking to the car. Mom was on my right side there was Angela. Which dragged my really good feelings back down the other side of the Ferris wheel.

I was rooted to the spot as firmly as if I’d licked a phone pole at -30 degrees. And that was just looking at Angela from a distance. But then, she caught my eye. And then started walking towards me!! I scrambled for something to say. I knew I wanted to apologize and just generally make some sort of a good impression. But it was like one of those tests where you knew all the answers a minute before the test but they were just out of reach leaving me with no clue what to do. It was the longest we ever held eye contact, and then she pulled something out of her stack of books and handed it to me. She said “You dropped this,” I think I might have said thanks but it really is all a blur. She then smiled and left without another word. I looked down and I was holding my conversation notes.

Mom standing next to me leaned down and asked “Who is that?” I decided to keep that answer to myself.

I went to class. People weren’t the kindest but I was kind to myself. It was a pretty average day all things considered. Which meant in the grand scheme of things. It would have been fabulous without the difficulties of the morning.

That night I picked up my conversation kit and held it remembering the time I’d worked on the questions. I was afraid of what it might hold. But when I opened the first page, and then the second and third all the way to the end, the conversation wasn’t empty. Each question had an answer in Angela’s wonderful handwriting. At the very end, she left these words. “I’d love to hang out ” PS. I like flowers.
