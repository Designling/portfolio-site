Novella · Chapter One

Fragments of Hope

By Aaron Ebeling

Souls cannot break, at least that is what the priests say. That acting in faith will bring joy, and not just despair. They tell me that there is hope that there is relief. That this too will pass. They say that the Almighty understands, that He has a plan that is beyond my understanding, and that all things will work for your good. That there are no accidents.

Absentmindedly Imana grabbed at a long stalk of grass pulling the seeds off, then slowly shredding it into smaller and smaller pieces that were carried away by the wind. It seemed like that was the story of her faith. The almighty had taken her spirit plucked like a long stem of grass away from its roots, and then slowly shredded her faith scattering her to the wind. It didn’t seem to have a purpose other than something to keep busy with while the time slowly passed. Leaving nothing to remain except the ever present wind.

The first three years of her pilgrimage imana had brimmed with hope and faith. The daily walks had kept her mind from wandering into thoughts about her parents and there had been a sense of purpose to the pilgrimage. She knew that the Almighty would use her for something great that the almighty would respect her sacrifice. She knew that bad things happened. But she also knew that miracles did as well. Every day she prayed that she would be granted a miracle that she would see her parents again and that there would be a plan for her life that it would somehow turn out.

The next four years imana wondered if she just wasn’t doing enough. Her prayers were sincere her pilgrimage was less strenuous since she was used to the walk. So she tried increasing how much she walked. But no matter what the sight of the city gates still was too daunting a process. The walls to closure seeming to grow larger each time she shied away from them. The pilgrimage still helped a little but why had she not yet been healed. Why had none of the promises come true. Imana drew away from people. She stopped responding to the friendly advances from the priests. The goal of a miracle consumed her and she became more alone.

Then one day as she had left her room to go on the pilgrimage, her mind swapped sides. And the thought she couldn’t get rid of was does anything I have done matter. If I don’t get the miracle what is the point. I could have done nothing and still been at this point. Tired, distressed, unhealed and angry, alone and forgotten. At what point should I have given up on the dream. Nothing is worth this much.

The last year had been the most difficult, motivation failed but she couldn’t think of anything else to do. Her home was at the temple. Imana had stopped making the pilgrimage more difficult and volunteered to help with the kitchens. But the main question on her mind. Imana just wondered if the Almighty even cared.

Imana flinched away from the sight of the city gates not daring to come any closer. But she still felt looking was important, at least it was all she could do at the moment. Her belief that someday she would walk through the gates and be strong enough to confront her past that she would heal had kept her going for a while. But that hope was merely a glimmer, faded over years of the problem not getting any better. She might have better days where the darkness did not linger but it always came back, and she was tired.

With slumped shoulders Imana turned towards the temple she completed the last leg of her self appointed pilgrimage. It was just her and the endless shifting grass. To her left stood Cadrophil, her home city which she had not entered in eight years. To her right, seemingly endless grass that stretched towards the horizon. It was not a very populated place, more of a way station for caravans and soldiers going to and from Zebudar - their next closest and largest city in the region. It stood as a bulwark protecting the land from the Fugazi.

Her second home, the temple, stood just ahead of her. Its walls were the only thing that didn’t shimmer as the wind blew through the tall grasslands. The white adobe walls covered in intricate patterns a slightly darker shade as dust collected in their grooves. Its most prominent feature was the chapel dome. It rose high above the grasslands but was an incomplete dome. It was as if someone had taken a gigantic knife and cut a piece leaving a sliver of space open to the elements. In the front of the temple there was a rounded courtyard with a well in the center with rooms branching all around it.

When she reached the temple courtyard there was no welcoming cry, just the cry of the wind. Not even a remembered was in sight. It had been too hot and dry to be out and about for weeks. The drought making everyone lethargic and slow to react. Imana walked into the washing room finding a pitcher of water sitting on the counter with a rain barrel next to it.

No water could be wasted, the barrel would catch the water she used to wash herself and would sit to let the dust settle before being given to the animals.

The dust on Imanas hands turned first to a thin sheet of mud which then fell off into an almost empty rain barrel. Shaking as much water off of her hands into the barrel she finished by drying her hands on the back of her dress. Her tired feet yearning for relief. she headed towards her bedroom hoping to catch a moment to rest before her evening chores and the sermon. Taking a last look at the temple courtyard her eyes fell on to the pitcher she had used for a drink and then to wash her hands. A sinking feeling in her gut urged her to go back and check to see if she had forgotten to place the lid back on the pitcher so no flies would gather around the water.

Her aching feet and mind seemed to have a mind of their own trying to continue down towards her room but with a supreme effort of will, Imana turned back towards the pitcher. It would have bothered her for hours had she not gone back. Coming closer she saw that indeed she had placed the lid back on the pitcher of water. Relieved, she moved back towards her room when a little voice in the back of her head said, “what if there isn’t enough water for the next person and they really need it?” It wasn’t much of a reason but still it didn’t feel right to leave without making sure that there would be enough for the next person.

Lifting the lid to the pitcher she checked that the water levels were just below the top. There was definitely room for more water but it would be sufficient for most needs. Imana clenched her scrunched fists and took a deep breath. Muttering under her breath that this did not matter. It would not matter. And yet she still went to the well with the pitcher.

Unceremoniously she dropped the bucket down the well where it fell for a few seconds before a splash echoed up from the depths. Making sure to gather a full bucket, she pulled on the rope hoisting it up. It came up cool and sweet, filling the pitcher to the brim.

Unsure about what to do with the rest of the water she was just about to pour it back down into the well and get to her room for some rest, when Reva called out

“Imana it is good to see you! How was the pilgrimage today?”

Clasping Imanas hands in her own, Reva looked into her eyes and sighed.

“There is a plan for you and I want to be there when you find it out. It will be ok… And it is a good thing you brought up water, if you are up for it, we need you in the kitchens, I am sure that serving will help you take your mind off things for a time.”

Sighing inwardly, Imana gave up plans for laying quietly in her room as she followed Reva though the temple to the kitchens. It seemed that Reva bent reality sometimes just by asking. Revas’ personality felt so humble and hopeful that even a little dose of reality should have brought it crashing down. But, Imana, having known Reva for eight years now, knew that reality had long since stopped trying and was just watching in astonishment as no matter what, Reva would solve any problem. Reva had reduced angry outbursts with a word; somehow produced enough food to feed everyone on a meager budget for years; and most astonishingly of all, had broken into Imana’s life through sheer persistent kindness.

The kitchen, busy as ever, filled with remembereds and kitchen aids hurrying about for the evening meal. There would be a thin vegetable curry and flatbread for dinner. With her bucket of water Reva assigned Imana to work making flatbread, giving her a friendly squeeze and whispering.

“If you need any help just let me know.”

Nodding in thanks, Imana placed the bucket of water on the ground and began mixing ingredients together. As she kneaded she overheard some of the kitchen aids.

“I just heard from him last week that their general was dismissed dishonorably and the army is retreating. It seems like the Fugazi are tired of the war. We should be ready to bring him home.”

Imana hoped that it would be so. It was one of her constant prayers on the pilgrimage, that the war would finish and they could have peace. Kneading and flattening out the dough, Imana thought about what she would do or what would change in her life if the war ended. She didn’t have any more family that could be affected by the war. It would probably not change much except having the priests take a larger interest in her. With so many others who were struggling there was little time to deal with the older traumas that were less likely to heal. Imana wasn’t sure she wanted the extra attention.

When she finished kneading she let the dough rise and worked on the batch of dough that had already risen. She pushed the dough through her fingers and squeezed out small bread balls which she then flattened and placed onto the wall of the oven. The hot oven transformed the dough into bread bubbling outwards giving it an uneven golden crust.

When Reva came around again, she assigned another helper to Imanas station, but they didn’t talk much. Imana didn’t know what to say and doubtless the other helper thought the otherwise. Occasionally a thought would pop into Imana’s head and she might take a breath to say it but then would hold herself back. Each time the thought made it worse and so it was best to stay silent. Besides the kitchen aid looked hopefully at a group laughing while they cooked in the cooler area of the kitchen.

As they worked a line of remembereds started to form. It was just about time for roll call. So imana stoppped kneading and cooking bread and stood at the end of the line. Each person would stand next to their remembered so the count could be verified from the day before and recorded for today. It was the way things were done. But for imana it was another reminder of how alone she was.

She didn’t have a remembered at least not anymore. When role was called they always singled her out at the end. Looked her in the eyes and asked “were you here yesterday” her reply was always the same as she looked down at the floor she would mutter “yes” and the ordeal would be over. But it just felt like she wasn’t trusted. Couldn’t they just use the role and check it daily instead of verifying.

That was when Jezic came into the kitchen the priest was in his fourties’ with grey hair starting to come in, he always seemed to be worrying about things. But it was his job to worry. He was in charge of keeping the temple ledgers and everything running smoothly. Mostly that involved food and how much help Reva was getting in the kitchen. The temple was given food from the city and passing caravans as it could not produce as much as they needed all by themselves.

He came down the line marking off names occasionally he would stop to say a word or two. When he made it to Imana, he looked down at her, gave her a bit of a smile as if to encourage her and asked. “We’re you here yesterday as well? Imana looked down her face burning. And said “yes” it was the best way to stay unnoticed.

Wishing for solitude and trying to finish more quickly Imana didn’t press the flatbread out as much as she should have and when she placed several in the oven they turned out either a little burnt on the top or a little doughy in the middle. So when Reva came around again she told Imana to get ready for the evening service.

Feeling a bit like a child but glad to leave the kitchen, Imana made her way through the halls to her room. It wasn’t really hers, nothing in the temple was. Whoever needed a place to stay could stay but it usually wasn’t for so long. It was at the end of the corridor in a quiet spot far away from the washroom, dining hall and chapel but less people came this way. Touching the incomplete circle in the door lintel she whispered “may I bring completeness” before entering. There was a small window that let in a cool draft. Quickly Imana changed into a clean and slightly more formal dress before laying on the bed for a little while. She enjoyed the stillness of the room but it wasn’t enough time to recover. So when the song of prayer called it woke her up and she was one of the last to come into the chapel.

Embarrassed, she sat in the last row close to the entrance and wall. It was a domed room with high Ceilings carved into white marble. Lines stretched in patterns in every direction. But the most prominent feature of the dome was the crack in the ceiling growing wider and wider until, at the base of the dome near the altar, there was enough space for a person to stand with their arms outstretched.

She remembered her experience in the completion ritual. She had walked up to the sliver in the temple dome with shaky steps. Every eye in the crowd following her and praying for her. It was after her parents had been killed, before even the truth that they would not be coming back. She had had faith that the Almighty, who was almighty, could bring her family back. The priests surely knew that was her desire. What else could an eight year old girl want after having lost her parents.

She remembered the priest leading her up to the sliver and then offering a bowl of water to dip her hands in. Then he instructed her to place her right hand on the edge of the temple wall so that she was facing the crowd and uncharacteristically she touched The cool stone, smooth from generations of people standing where she stood. She could imagine her father and mother sitting in the front row with their armor on looking with tears in their eyes as she performed this rite. But they weren’t there. Not even with their remembered.

Next she placed her left hand on the other side of the sliver cut out of the temple, again marveling that the stone was so smooth and weathered. Imana closed her eyes concentrating wholly on what she wanted. She wanted her parents back again. She wanted her suffering to mean something. Then according to the ritual she said in the loudest voice she could muster. “May I bring completeness”. It sounded so weak, even amplified by the acoustics of the room, her voice quavering and on the edge of tears. She opened her eyes and saw that everyone was on their feet. The crowd repeated with a thunderous roar “May we bring completeness”.

The sound reverberated off of the walls surrounding her and she could even feel the dome tremble with power as tears filled her eyes. She knew her wish would be fulfilled. A fire of knowledge that she would see her parents that she would matter, that she would protect and that she would feel joy kindled within her.

She often thought back on that moment suspended in time. Mistrusting that it even happened. Thinking that the feelings were more due to her excitement about the possibility or situation rather than a promise from the Almighty.

It seemed that it was all just a lie. There was no joy, at least not now. Perhaps it was for others but not herself. At first she had hoped that joy would come back into her life that she might matter or that she might protect. But across time those were all sucked away into the void.

She looked up at the ceiling wondering what she could do to find personal peace. It seemed like nothing would help. And continuing to go on without seeing any results left her weak. Of course she could live at the temple, she could probably find a life here but was that all that she could ever be. Where so often she hoped to just be ignored. To not be a project for someone else.

The sermon seemed to go on for a long time. Pleading she asked in her mind “Please. Please let me be able to deal with this. Let me forget or shield me. Please help me to find peace. Straining to feel she sank into herself. Feeling a lump around her heart. A sponge deadening anything that came close to it. Her mind circled it and tried to take it in. The priest finished his remarks and still the feeling of peace held back from Imana. It felt like she could either feel ok in her heart or in her mind. But never both at the same time.

Then the priest beckoned everyone to come up to the front and receive a blessing and Imana rose along with the crowd. When it was their rows turn, she flowed into the corridor leading to the front. But leaving her row, her foot snagged on the bench and she fell forwards knocking into an elderly woman. Who stumbled with a cry to the floor.

Unsure of what to do, Imana reached down to pull the woman up. Imanas face burned as she apologized profusely which disturbed the quiet even more. The old woman’s eyes didn’t betray anything against her. But Imana still felt like it would have been better for the floor to swallow her whole. But she couldn’t run. It was in the middle of the sermon. So she waited head bowed till it was her turn to be washed.

When she did finally get back to her room after a hasty meal, tears fell on her pillow as she struggled to find sleep.
