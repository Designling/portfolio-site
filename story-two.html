Short Story

The Leper

By Aaron Ebeling

Zalah scanned the moonlit beach, wary of late-night stragglers. Seeing no one, she gripped her crutch tightly and made her way down to the water.

The seawater was cold, but she barely reacted, having performed her washing ritual for five years. The washing helped loosen and remove deadend skin but it couldn’t stop the curse from spreading.

The curse began on her leg as a small white patch. But she convinced herself it was nothing. When lumps began to appear on her arms, she resorted to wearing long sleeves to maintain the facade that everything was well. Surely, it would all vanish in a month, and telling anyone would only make everyone anxious. By the time the rouse was up her whole leg was as white as snow.

She shivered as she finished washing, but still waited in the icy water till she scanned the shoreline again for signs of life. Seeing no one, she made her way to the discarded fishing nets. Her wet ragged clothes clung to her, leaching her of what little warmth she had. Searching the nets she found two fish that she stowed in a pocket for later. Taking one last look toward the shore she started as she noticed a boat on the water. As she watched, it came closer and Zalah fled.

Her cave, perched high on a cliff near the beach, was difficult to reach, especially with a crutch but the privacy was well worth it. Zalah wasn’t sure how many people knew of her secret haven, but she’d remained undisturbed in the four years she’d lived there.

As she ascended the steep slope, Zalah paused to catch her breath, leaning heavily on her crutch. The sight of the approaching boat spurred her on. Finally reaching the mouth of her small cave, she glanced back to see the boat, entering the cove.

In the cave, Zalah’s meager supplies included a bit of food, a clam shell serving as a knife, and a small blanket. Retrieving the shell and blanket, she returned to the cave’s entrance to watch the boat.

She wrapped herself up and slowly cleaned and ate the two fish. She watched as the small boat, beached in the sand on the far side of the cove and figures quickly disembarked. They didn’t light a fire but did set up several small tents where they presumably fell asleep. She watched the tents and wondered who would be coming to this cove in the sea of Galeliee?

Having finished her meal and having watched the boat and tents for several hours without anything interesting happening, Zalah tidied up, went to the back of her cave and curled up in her blanket for warmth.

The following day, the beach teamed with an unusual number of people, and more continued to arrive. Hundreds of people were on the beach, their collective noise a low roar punctuated by spikes of excitement. The entire crowd centering around the boat from the previous night.

Zalah observed for a while, puzzled by the intense interest in such a vessel. It didn’t seem capable of carrying anyone of importance. Yet, the influx of people persisted. As she studied the crowd’s movements, she noticed their focus on a single man. He walked among them, and wherever he stopped, people clustered around him.

The commotion continued until noon, when the man, after meeting with people for some time, made his way to the shoreline. In a voice that barely reached Zalah’s ears, he shouted, “Are there any sick or injured who need healing? Please come, and I will heal them.”

At first, Zalah struggled to comprehend his words. They seemed too incredible to be true. Yet, the crowd reacted as if they had anticipated this invitation. As soon as the man spoke, people began moving purposefully through the crowd.

The first to approach was a mother carrying a small girl. The child was gently handed over to the man, who held her in his arms. He stood with her for a minute his head bowed and then in an instant the girl leaped from his arms, gave him a hug and jumped into her mothers arms full of energy and joy. The crowd erupted in cheers.

Once he was done, another person in need stepped forward. It went on and on, some limping, others carried, yet all were able to walk away rejoicing. It was difficult to believe, almost dreamlike. The exuberant joy of the crowd below lifted Zalah’s spirits, and she couldn’t help but smile and raise her fist in triumph. She felt an urge to leap out in praise. It was as if a prophet of old had returned!

As the line dwindled, the man’s voice echoed once more.

“Is there anyone else who needs healing? Please come, and I will heal you.”

This time, the message struck Zalah. Was he, the prophet, speaking directly to her? Inviting her to be healed? Surely, if he could heal others, he could heal her. With a surge of hope she hadnt felt in years Zalah fumbled for her crutch gripped it and with exuberance, pushed herself to her feet.

As Zalah took her first exultant step out of the darkness of the cave and into the bright sunlight, the crutch snapped with a sickening crack, splitting in half.

She tumbled forward, rocks digging into her hands as she cried out in pain and surprise.

When she spotted the two halves her heart dropped. A short moan escaped her lips.

“No…no, no! No no no no please… please!” she said in a desperate whisper as she grabbed both ends and tried to force them together, praying that sheer will could somehow mend the break.

But after a minute, her hands trembled and the crutch fell apart.

Zalah stared down at the two pieces in disbelief, for the crutch to have lasted so long, and have been so consistent. But it had shattered almost as if God had cursed it.

By the time she regained her composure, the prophet was already climbing into the boat.

She had waited too long.

She hadn’t processed the loss quickly enough.

The boat began to move away, propelled by the rhythmic strokes of oars.

Tears welled up in Zalah’s eyes. She shook the broken halves of her cane in silent fury, wanting to scream but afraid of the crowd below. She had been too slow, too hesitant. Hope had been offered, only to be snatched away.

But then, the boat stopped moving.

The oars were lifted from the water.

An anchor splashed.

And the prophet spoke.

His words, carried by the water, reached her cave as a whisper, yet were clear enough to understand. Zalah lay back, resting her head against the cool stone wall, her eyes closed, out of breath and overwhelmed.

Every person who had approached the prophet had been healed. He had invited them, not just once, but twice.

Zalah considered going down, interrupting the sermon even if she had to crawl. The crowd was immense, and without her crutch, reaching the prophet would be nearly impossible.

To be close to so many people, without a line of retreat seeing their pitying judgmental looks she couldn’t do it. But to be healed. Was the invitation still extended to her? The time for healing seemed to have passed, and the prophet would surely have to move on, to places where she could not follow.

He couldn’t stay, and nobody knew where she lived.

The loss of her crutch left her stranded. How would she move around now? Her only option seemed to be crawling down to the beach and hoping to find a suitable piece of driftwood to replace it. She had no tools to shape or cut anything new.

Zalah attempted to listen to the sermon, but concentration proved elusive. The gentle sound of the waves and the prophet’s faint voice lulled her into a state of distraction. She closed her eyes, straining to focus, but soon gave up, letting her mind wander through familiar paths.

The clatter of rocks near her cave jolted Zalah from her thoughts. Grabbing a piece of her broken crutch for protection, she inched deeper into the shadows.

Her heart pounded in her chest, and she struggled to silence her breathing as a small girl appeared in the cave’s entrance. The girl peered into the darkness.

The girl let out a sigh, followed by a yelp as she caught sight of Zalah lurking in the back of the cave.

Zalah immediately said “Go away!” In a harsh whisper.

The child raised her hands but didn’t retreat. After a brief hesitation, instead of leaving, the little girl took a step forward.

“Get back! Don’t come near me!” Zalah shouted.

But the child took another step forward, undeterred.

Zalah’s voice rose to a panicked shriek. “Get back! Get back! Back! I am cursed!”

The child finally stopped advancing, then turned and scampered away.

Zalah retched, her fingers trembling as she tried to calm herself. No harm had been done. After waiting for her heart to stop pounding, she slowly edged toward the cave entrance to ensure the child had gone home. But halfway there, she spotted the girl again.

“Get away! Please! Go away!” Zalah cried out again, her voice filled with desperation.

The girl momentarily disappeared, but as Zalah waited, she reappeared, and the girl was of all things smiling.

“Why are you smiling? You should go. Your parents must be worried. You need to leave. You can’t be around me. Please,” Zalah pleaded.

But the little girl remained steadfast, staring intently at Zalah. Then, with innocent curiosity, asked, “What happened to your skin? Why is it all white and flaky?”

Zalah tried again, her voice softer now. “I am a leper, I am unclean and you should leave right now.”

But the child remained unfazed. “What’s your name? How did you get that way? Did it hurt? Does it hurt now?”

Still wary, Zalah leaned back against the cave wall, her breath coming in shallow gasps. It had been so long since anyone had spoken to her, let alone asked her questions.

Zalah remained silent, hoping the child would leave if she simply ignored her. But the tactic failed when the child crept closer.

“Get back, please. You don’t want this. I will tell you what you want to know if you go back…. And never come back,” Zalah offered, her voice trembling slightly.

The child nodded in agreement.

After a moment’s hesitation, Zalah continued, “Most of the time it doesn’t hurt. I mostly just can’t feel anything, I know I should be able to feel but I cant and its terrifying.”

“What about my other questions? What’s your name? And how did you get leprosy?”

The answer felt like a long time coming. “my name is Zalah… I must have done something bad, to be given this curse. They said it was a punishment but I dont know what I did except hide that I was cursed”

“Why would God curse you?”

“Because… I waited too long. Now please go.”

And then the girl scampered off out of the cave and out of sight.

Zalah moved to the cave entrance to ensure that the girl had really gone. The sun was bright but at the mouth of the cave, the voice of the prophet was clear enough to hear. The girl was nowhere to be seen.

Zalah, keeping a watchful eye out for the girl, listened intently to the prophet’s words.

“A shepherd who has one hundred sheep, if he loses one of them, will leave the ninety-nine and go after the lost sheep until he finds it. He would surely put it on his shoulders and bring it back home. He would rejoice that the lost sheep was found.”

“Put another way,” the prophet continued, “a woman who has ten coins and loses one wouldn’t she clean the whole house and search until she finds it? And when she has found it, share the good news with her friends and neighbors that what was lost is found.”

“It is the same in heaven over one person who repents.”

“So it is the same with people. There was a man who had two sons. One asked that he should receive his share of the inheritance before the death of his father. And the father gave it to him. The son left and went to another country, where he lost all the wealth of his father. Then a famine came and the son was reduced to pig farming and wished he could just eat the husks given to the pigs.”

“Is the son still loved? Is he not still a son?”

“After a while, the boy came to himself and made the journey back. He didn’t come back expecting to be called a son, just to be a servant.”

“And when the father spotted his son in the distance, he did not wait for him to come. He ran out to meet him, hugged him, and shouted praise.”

“The son said to his father. Father, I have sinned against heaven, and against you, and am no more worthy to be called your son.”

“But the father said to his servants: bring out the best robe and put a ring on his finger and slaughter the fatted calf and rejoice that my son has returned. My son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found.”

“You are the children of God. The children of Abraham are the children of the covenant. Come unto me. Come and be found.” the prophet’s voice proclaimed.

Tears streamed down Zalah’s face as she listened to the stories. Even though she struggled to believe them fully. Of course, the shepherd and the woman would be happy to recover their lost property. But would her husband welcome her back? Surely not. Would her father? Would he truly welcome home a leper?

The weight of her past mistakes bore down on Zalah, each one seemingly worse than the last. Yet, if the prophet’s words were to be believed, he was still inviting her. Inviting her to come home, to be found, to be healed as he had healed others. She knew she didn’t deserve it, but as day turned into night, she decided she would try.

The prophet preached throughout the day and into the evening, and the crowd remained. They surrounded him constantly, or he would venture out onto the water to be heard more clearly. He often spoke in parables, or at least that’s all Zalah could make out from her distance. But each story, though simple, touched her heart. She didn’t understand all of them, but this was no pharisee or scribe lecturing on the law.

Throughout the day, a battle raged within Zalah—hope vying with her deep-seated sense of uncleanness. But by nightfall, as the crowd settled into slumber, her decision was made.

She ventured out of her cave toward the prophet’s tent, moving on hands and knees in a shuffling gait. Progress was slow the steep incline demanding her full concentration. Every few feet she would look up to check that the coast was still relatively clear. By the time she made it to the beach, The moon had already risen and it was far later than she’d hoped.

Hundreds of people slept soundly, unaware of her presence just a few feet away. She crawled to the water’s edge, the cold waves briefly revitalizing her bruised and weary body. Following the shoreline, she made her way to the prophet’s tent.

Upon reaching it, she found no guards. It wasn’t a grand tent, just a well-worn one. She knelt infront of the tent her heart beating madly, and said “hello.”

Her voice cracked.

She cleared her throat and said “hello” again as loud as she dared. Nothing happened, she waited hoping that somehow the prophet would have heard her. But after what felt like eternity the tent flap was still shut.

Zalah took a deep breath, looked around, gathered her courage and touched the fabric of the tent, but hesitated to push it open. The scene felt strangely familiar. Her heart pounded in her chest as she tightened her grip on the fabric and slowly, pushed it out of the way.

Inside, the tent was shrouded in darkness. As Zalah’s eyes adjusted, she discerned a mound that gradually took on the shape of the prophet. He slept peacefully, his arm serving as a pillow, a blanket draped over him. Her breath quickened and she felt the sand shifting beneath her knees as she moved into the tent.

Upon closer inspection, she noticed dark circles under the man’s eyes. Yet, he slept with a profound sense of serenity. But his eyes remained closed, his breath steady and rhythmic.

Zalah’s hands trembled as she clutched her clothes tighter around herself. It was the closest she had come to another person in four years. She trembled again as a whisper escaped her lips, “Oh God, please help me.” The man didn’t stir, and so she stretched out her shaking hand. But the sight of the dead patches on her skin and the lump on her wrist, reaching towards someone so seemingly perfect, made her hesitate. She could feel the warmth radiating from him, she was so close, but the scene was too familiar.

Four years ago, she had moved through her house at night, her entire body shaking. That evening, she had scrubbed her leg raw so much that it bled, she didn’t even notice the pain. She just knew that underneath it there was even more uncleanness. It was the realization that this problem wasn’t just going to fall away.

For months, she had been consumed by stress. The patches of numbness had spread, and lumps were appearing on her body that she could no longer ignore. She had been terrified to speak about it, afraid her worst fears would be confirmed. She prayed with all her might that she wasn’t cursed and unclean, that it was just a temporary ailment caused by the weather, the sea air, her clothes, or her food, that everything would be okay. But it wasn’t, and the fear of the unknown had become overwhelming.

Her husband was asleep when she finally arrived home that night. She walked through the quiet empty house till she came to their cot where he slept so peacefully never knowing that he was next to a leper. It took minutes that felt like hours for her to gather the courage to reach out to him. She clutched her shaul desperately her hands trying to keep herself together. But she had let go, reached out, and gently woken him.

When he saw her arm, her husband had been concerned, but when he saw her leg, he recoiled in horror, scrambling away from her as if she were a monster. Tears had streamed down Zalah’s face, but he hadn’t comforted her. She wanted to say something anything, but what could she say. She has wanted him to understand her pain but what could he have said. And that look of revulsion stayed with her. Her husband returned an hour later with a priest, finding her still frozen in the same position, as if carved from stone.

Zalah choked back a sob as she hovered, her hand over the sleeping prophet, the one who claimed he could heal her. Then, with a sudden movement, she closed her hand into a fist and brought it to her chest, clutching it tightly as silent tears streamed down her face. She couldn’t do it. She couldn’t bring herself to touch someone who wasn’t unclean like her.

Overwhelmed, she fled the tent as quickly as she could, feeling every grain of sand digging into her hands and knees as she made her way back to the sanctuary of her cave imagining the horrified look the prophet would have had if she had woken him.

Zalah woke late in the morning with a heavy heart. Her hands and knees were scraped and bruised, her body ached all over.

Why had she attempted the impossible? What was a wild hope if not a cruel illusion? But she had to admit, it was her own inability to reach out for help that had held her back. Her best effort simply hadn’t been enough.

Then, a distant sound caught her attention. A child’s laughter echoed into the cave, far too close for comfort. Zalah instinctively crouched down, pressing herself against the wall. She heard another giggle and a joyful exclamation. Then came the man’s voice, asking, “Where did you see her?”

“Just right over here,” said the child’s voice. “We should see it in a moment.”

A figure stepped into the cave entrance. Then stopped to come into the cave waving a hand back to the child. Zalah’s heart pounded in her chest, her face flushed and her hands trembled.

Zalah stammered, her voice trembling, “Get back, I am unclean, I am cursed.”

He came closer, reaching out a hand that gently clasped hers. Zalah instinctively tried to pull away, but his grip was firm yet gentle. He brought his other hand to cup her bruised and torn fingers, his touch steady and unwavering.

Emotions surged within her, threatening to overwhelm her, but his hands remained a rock in the storm, their unwavering pressure keeping her anchored in the moment.

And then, his voice, gentle and reassuring, broke through the silence. “Zalah, I know you.”

“I’m so sorry,” she sobbed. “I am unclean, I have made so many mistakes.” The calluses on his hands gently soothed the scrapes and bruises on hers.

“I forgive you,” he said simply.

Disbelief turned into indescribable relief that washed over her like a tidal wave, crashing into her past, transforming the broken landscape into something beautiful. It was as if she were being born anew, filled with a lightness she hadn’t felt in years.

She looked into his eyes, and on his wonderful, lined, weathered, and bearded face was a radiant smile.

With outstretched arms, he beckoned her. “Come! And let me heal you!”

Still holding tightly to the savior’s hand, Zalah rose to her feet. As a wave of warmth washed over her, the scrapes and bruises on her hands and knees vanished, and a tingling sensation coursed through her leg. They embraced and she clung to him tears streaming down their faces.

She didn’t know how long she stood there, encircled in his arms, but their embrace was interrupted by a tug on her threadbare robe.

It was the child, beckoning her out of the cave into the bright sunlight of a glorious new day.
